**20 Smaller, Casual & Shorter Q&A**
(Perfect for quick chats, LinkedIn posts, or casual interview warm-ups. Light, real, and straight from the builder life.)

**1. Q: Why write premflow in C instead of Python or Rust?**
**A:** Because I open it many times every single day. C starts instantly with zero runtime. No waiting, no thinking — just flow. As it grew past a tiny script I put it on elomaxz (C MVU) so features could scale without becoming a slow, heavy stack. Sometimes the “old” language plus a clear framework is the simplest win.

**2. Q: What’s your favorite daily tweak on Arch Linux with Omarchy?**
**A:** The self-healing profile system. One command and my whole workstation (ROCm, Kubernetes, security audits) rebuilds exactly how I like it. Feels like having a personal sysadmin that never sleeps.

**3. Q: How did building Grok Dia actually change your workflow?**
**A:** I can now ask you about any webpage with full context in one click. Research that used to take 10 tabs and copy-paste now happens in seconds. Small tool, massive time back in my day.

**4. Q: Tell me the Zod story in 30 seconds.**
**A:** Found a sneaky ordering bug in nullish chaining that broke complex schemas. Fixed it in public (PR #1702). Thousands of projects got quieter. Best feeling: fixing something that annoyed me every week.

**5. Q: Quick win from the big TypeScript migration at Oneflow?**
**A:** 70% fewer type errors and a custom script that saved 200+ hours of manual work. We kept git history intact. Automation that actually paid for itself in weeks.

**6. Q: What was the “epic predictor” in your 2016 thesis?**
**A:** A smart cloud system that predicted device energy use using real user behavior. Nine years later it still feels like the right way to build agentic AI — respect the battery and the human behind the screen.

**7. Q: How does writing Tamil poetry help your engineering?**
**A:** It trains me to find the clearest metaphor for complex ideas. Same skill I use when explaining system architecture or onboarding flows. Poetry and code are both about making the invisible obvious.

**8. Q: Tool you literally cannot live without right now?**
**A:** premflow. Notes, tasks, pomodoros, daily review — all in one C binary I open without thinking. It grew with elomaxz under it, but it still feels instant.

**9. Q: Most satisfying open-source moment?**
**A:** Shipping the react-intl Babel plugin as a real npm package. It killed duplicate translation keys across the whole project. Personal annoyance → tool that helped the whole team.

**10. Q: Why automate your CV pipeline with Rust + GitHub Actions?**
**A:** I was tired of manually compiling LaTeX and uploading PDFs. Now I just commit and get a clean PDF + public S3 link + PR for review. One less thing stealing brain cycles.

**11. Q: Favorite current AI experiment?**
**A:** My local “Ask Me Anything About Me” RAG chatbot. Runs on Ollama + Chroma. Zero cost, remembers things I forgot I wrote. Feels like having a second brain that stays private.

**12. Q: How do you balance building while living with parents and your son?**
**A:** Short focused blocks. 90 minutes of deep work, then family time. The tools I build (premflow especially) help me protect that family time instead of stealing it.

**13. Q: Simplest productivity hack that actually stuck?**
**A:** Ending every day with a 2-minute review in premflow. What moved the needle? What drained energy? Takes almost no time but keeps me honest.

**14. Q: Hobby that unexpectedly taught you engineering lessons?**
**A:** Explaining algorithms with Tamil literary metaphors on my old blog. Taught me that the best abstractions feel natural, not clever. Same rule applies to API design.

**15. Q: What does “quiet infrastructure” mean to you?**
**A:** Tools so good you forget they exist. arch-machine, premflow, latex-cv — they just work. The best tech disappears into the background and lets you focus on the actual work.

**16. Q: Mac Mini or Arch Linux for daily driving?**
**A:** Arch for serious work (full control, Omarchy profiles). Mac Mini for quick creative stuff and when I want zero setup friction. Best of both worlds.

**17. Q: Favorite memory from your Oneflow team lead days?**
**A:** Watching the team go from “we need approval for everything” to self-organizing in under a year. Playwright rewrite + culture shift = way fewer late-night fires.

**18. Q: How do you decide what to open source?**
**A:** If it solves my problem and I think it might save someone else the same headache — I ship it. If it’s too personal or half-baked, it stays private. Simple filter.

**19. Q: One lesson from your 2016 energy work that still guides you?**
**A:** Small changes in the right place create outsized impact. Don’t optimize everything — find the 20% that eats 80% of the energy (or attention) and fix that first.

**20. Q: One thing you’re quietly excited to build next?**
**A:** A personal agent that actually understands my context across all my tools without sending data anywhere. Local-first, energy-aware, and boringly reliable. The kind of thing I wish existed in 2016.
